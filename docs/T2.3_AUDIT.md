## 0. Scope of T2.3

T2.3 concerns **only** the per-packet consistency design invariant: each data-plane
packet is processed by exactly one process term (encoding one flow-table
configuration) throughout its flight.

T2.3 is documented (per `PROOF_OBLIGATIONS.md:169-180`) as a **design invariant**
- it is stated in the paper as design rationale (§II-B), not as a derived theorem
with a formal proof in the paper body. The audit therefore checks whether the
Lean implementation (a) defines the invariant, and (b) either proves or
explicitly states it as an assumption, in a form faithful to the paper.

Per-packet consistency is **not** bisimilarity (T4.x), nor is it the
ground-completeness or safety theorems. It is a semantic-layering/design
property stated at the operational-semantics level. T2.3 depends on T2.2
(operational semantics) and, transitively, T2.1 (DyNetKAT syntax).

## 1. Paper specification (per-packet consistency)

**Paper location:** printed p. 8–9, §II (§II-B "Operational Semantics"). The
statement appears as a design rationale paragraph in the introduction of the
operational semantics; it is *not* a numbered theorem. (`PROOF_OBLIGATIONS.md:169`
records it as a design invariant; `PAPER_CONFORMANCE.md:29` records the same;
`PLAN.md:39` mislabels it "Theorem 3" - there is no Theorem 3 in the paper for
per-packet consistency; the paper only *motivates* the design via this property.)

**Mathematical statement (reconstructed from §II-B):**

> The semantics is designed so that each data-plane packet sees exactly one set
> of flow tables throughout its flight.

Equivalently (per `PAPER_CONFORMANCE.md:200`): each packet in the pending list
`H` is processed by exactly one NetKAT policy (flow-table configuration
encoded by the process term `d`) before reaching the handled list `H′`. The
configuration `(d, H, H′)` carries a *single* `d` governing all packets in `H`;
there is no mutable per-packet table mapping threaded through `(d, H, H′)` -
that is the journal-extension artifact that was explicitly rejected
(`PAPER_CONFORMANCE.md:102-103`, `DESIGN_ALARMS.md:A1`).

**Paper evidence for the design rationale (from §II-B):**
- Printed p. 9: configurations are `(d, H, H′)` with a single process term `d`.
- The transition rules of Fig. 8 advance `d` atomically per packet (e.g. the
  `(cpolX;)` rule consumes `σ` from `H`, runs `⟦N⟧(σ)`, and pushes `σ′` to
  `H′`, all under the *same* `d`-structure minus the consumed prefix).
- No rule mutates a table; reconfiguration appears only as the `rcfg(x,r)`
  action, which swaps the *single* `d` synchronously (preserving one
  configuration-wide `d` per step).

**Assumptions & side conditions:**
- Finite packet domain (finite field names, finite values) - `⟦N⟧` is a finite
  relation.
- Each packet processed by exactly one `d` per transition step.

**Required Lean representation (per `PAPER_CONFORMANCE.md:213`):**
A theorem or invariant witnessing that the `Step` relation preserves the
property that a single process term `d` governs each packet's flight. As a
*design invariant* (not a derived theorem), the faithful formalization must at
minimum *state* it as an assumption alongside T2.2.

## 7. Verdict

**T2.3: BLOCKED.**

Nothing that T2.3 requires exists in the Lean repo:

- The **host type** for a process term `d` (DyNetKAT syntax, T2.1) is absent, so
  there is no `d` to govern packet processing.
- The **configuration** `(d, H, H′)` (T2.2) is absent, so there is no state in
  which per-packet consistency could even be stated.
- The **transition relation** `Step` (T2.2) and its **labels** (T2.2) are
  absent, so there is no dynamics whose preservation of the single-`d`
  invariant could be proved or assumed.
- The **paper's design-invariant statement** of per-packet consistency is not
  reproduced anywhere in `lean/`.

The only available prerequisite is the NetKAT denotational relation
`⟦N⟧(σ,τ)` (`Semantics.lean:61-71`), which is *used by* the `(cpolX;)` rule's
premise `σ′ ∈ ⟦N⟧(σ)` but has nothing DyNetKAT-scale to be applied to.

Because T2.3 is a **design invariant stated in the paper's rationale** (not a
numbered theorem), the minimum faithful formalization is: (1) implement T2.1 +
T2.2, and (2) either prove the invariant from the `Step` rule structure or state
it explicitly as an assumption bundled with the `Step` definition, per
`PROOF_OBLIGATIONS.md:169`. Until T2.1 and T2.2 exist, T2.3 cannot pass.
